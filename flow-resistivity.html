Flow resistivity

Flow resistivity (σ) is the most important property of a porous absorber. Use this tool to calculate σ from a measurement, or to estimate σ from the fibre diameter and the porosity.

How to use

  1. Select the method.
  2. For a measurement, type the pressure difference, the air velocity and the sample thickness.
  3. For an estimate, type the fibre diameter and the porosity (or the densities).
  4. Click "Use in porous absorber calculator" to send the value to that page.

Description

Air can flow through a porous material, but the material resists the flow. Flow resistivity is the pressure difference across a sample, divided by the air velocity and the sample thickness. The unit is Pa·s/m² (also written rayl/m or N·s/m⁴).

A measured value is always better than an estimate. ISO 9053 gives the measurement method. Many manufacturers give σ on the data sheet.

Porosity is the volume of air in the material divided by the total volume. For mineral wool it is usually between 0.92 and 0.99. If you know the bulk density of the product and the density of the fibre material, porosity = 1 − (bulk density / fibre density).

Method

Measurement: σ = Δp / (U d) Bies & Hansen: σ = 27.3 (ρ_m/ρ_f)^1.53 η / d_f² Parallel to fibres: σ = 3.94 η (1−ε)^1.413 [1 + 27 (1−ε)³] / (a² ε) Perpendicular (a 6–10 µm): σ = 10.56 η (1−ε)^1.531 / (a² ε³) Perpendicular (a 20–30 µm): σ = 6.8 η (1−ε)^1.296 / (a² ε³) Random orientation: σ = (4η/a²) [0.55 (1−ε)^(4/3) / ε + √2 (1−ε)² / ε³] Random, glass fibre: σ = 3.2 η (1−ε)^1.42 / a² Random, mineral fibre: σ = 4.4 η (1−ε)^1.59 / a²

d_f is the fibre diameter, a is the fibre radius, ε is the porosity and η is the dynamic viscosity of air. The fibre formulas are from Cox & D'Antonio (table 5.1, after Mechel and Tarnow). They were checked against the "Porous Absorber Calculator V1.60" spreadsheet. The spreadsheet gives 7 075 for random orientation because it rounds the exponent 4/3 to 1.333. This tool uses 4/3 and gives 7 065.

Sources: definition and measurement ; Bies & Hansen ; fibre formulas ; check values .

Limits

  • The estimates are for fibrous materials only. Do not use them for foam.
  • The fibre diameter in a real product is not constant. The estimate can be wrong by a factor of 2 or more. Use a measured value for final design.
  • The two "perpendicular" formulas are for fibre radii of 6–10 µm and 20–30 µm. The tool uses the first formula up to a radius of 15 µm and the second formula above 15 µm. Between 10 µm and 20 µm, no formula has a stated range.
  • Binder, facings and compression change the flow resistivity.
