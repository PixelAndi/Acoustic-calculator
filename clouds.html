Ceiling clouds, tilted clouds and baffles

This calculator gives the sound absorption of porous panels that hang in a room: horizontal clouds, tilted clouds and vertical baffles. The result is the equivalent absorption area of each object in m².

How to use

  1. Select the type: horizontal cloud, tilted cloud or vertical baffle.
  2. Type the size and thickness of one panel, and the flow resistivity of the material.
  3. For a cloud, type the distance from the ceiling to the top of the panel. For a tilted cloud, type the tilt angle. This is the distance at the high edge.
  4. Type the quantity.
  5. Read the equivalent absorption area per object and in total. Use the total in the reverberation calculator.

Description

A cloud is a panel that hangs below the ceiling. Sound reaches its lower face, its edges and, through the gap, its upper face. A baffle hangs vertically. Sound reaches both of its faces.

A tilted cloud has a gap that increases from one edge to the other. The tilt also changes the direction of the reflection from the panel. A reflection from a panel that is tilted by φ goes 2φ from its usual direction. Use this to send sound to the audience, or away from a wall.

The absorption of an object is given as an equivalent absorption area A (m²), not as α. Laboratories measure hanging objects in the same way (ISO 354).

Method

The calculator gives two models. Both use the diffuse-field (Paris) integral and the Delany & Bazley or Miki model for the porous material.

Model A – ceiling behind the cloud (one face, with air gap): A = S · α_A, α_A = layer + air gap g + rigid ceiling Tilted cloud: the panel is divided into 8 strips; each strip has its own gap g_i; α_A is the mean of the strip values. Model B – free in the room (both faces): A = 2 S · α_free, α_free = 1 − |R|² − |t|² (transfer matrix, air on both sides) S = area of one face of the panel.

Model A is a good estimate for a cloud near the ceiling, where little sound reaches the upper face from the sides. Model B is for baffles and for clouds far from the ceiling. Measured values can be higher than both models, because of the edge effect.

Limits

  • Neither model includes the edges. Real panels absorb more per square metre than an infinite layer, because sound bends into the edges (edge effect). Small panels have a larger edge effect.
  • The strip method for a tilted cloud is an approximation.
  • Baffles that hang close together shield each other. The model does not include this.
  • The model does not include a fabric that touches only the lower face, frames or supports.
  • For a final design, use values measured on the product (ISO 354).

References: T. J. Cox & P. D'Antonio, Acoustic Absorbers and Diffusers, chapters 2 and 5. ISO 354, measurement of sound absorption in a reverberation room (objects).
