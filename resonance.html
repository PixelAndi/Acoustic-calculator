Resonance and cavity depth

Use this tool for a quick design. It gives the resonance frequency of a resonant absorber. It can also give the cavity depth that is necessary for a target frequency.

How to use

  1. Select the absorber type.
  2. Select what you want to find: the frequency or the cavity depth.
  3. Type the panel data. Then type the cavity depth or the target frequency.
  4. Read the result. The chart shows how the frequency changes with the cavity depth.
  5. Use the full calculator page of the same absorber to see the absorption curve.

Description

A resonant absorber has a mass in front of an air spring. The mass is the air in the holes or slots, or the sheet. The spring is the air in the cavity. The resonance frequency is the frequency of maximum movement. Most absorption occurs near this frequency.

Method

Perforated or slotted: f₀ = (c/2π) √(ε / (t′ d)) d = ε c² / (ω₀² t′) Membrane, lumped: f₀ = (1/2π) √(ρ₀c² / (m d)) d = ρ₀c² / (ω₀² m) Membrane, exact: ω m = ρ₀c cot(ω d / c) d = (c/ω₀) arctan(ρ₀c / (ω₀ m))

t′ is the thickness of the panel with the end corrections. The end corrections are the same as on the perforated and slotted pages. ε is the open area and m is the surface mass of the sheet.

Sources: Helmholtz resonance and end corrections ; membrane resonance .

Limits

  • The lumped formulas are correct only if the cavity is much smaller than the wavelength. The tool shows a warning if the cavity depth is more than 1/12 of the wavelength.
  • A porous absorber in the cavity lowers the resonance frequency a little. The formulas are for an empty cavity.
  • The membrane formula is for a limp sheet. A stiff sheet has a higher resonance frequency.
  • All formulas are for normal incidence.
