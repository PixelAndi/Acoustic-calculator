Sound in air and wavelength

This tool gives the properties of air and the wavelength of sound. It also gives the lowest frequency at which a layer of a given depth is a quarter wavelength.

Inputs

Results

Wavelength by octave band

How to use

  1. Type the air temperature and the air pressure.
  2. Type a frequency to get its wavelength.
  3. Type the depth of an absorber (with its air gap) to get its quarter-wave frequency.

Description

The speed of sound in air increases with temperature. The wavelength is the speed of sound divided by the frequency.

Near a rigid wall the air cannot move. The air moves most at a quarter wavelength from the wall. A porous absorber works best where the air moves most. Thus, as a guide, a porous absorber works well above the frequency where its total depth is a quarter wavelength. Use the porous absorber page for an accurate result.

Method

c = √(γ R T) γ = 1.4, R = 287.05 J/(kg·K), T in kelvin ρ₀ = p / (R T) Z₀ = ρ₀ c η = 1.458×10⁻⁶ T^1.5 / (T + 110.4) (Sutherland) λ = c / f f(λ/4 = d) = c / (4 d)

Limits

  • The air is dry. Humidity increases the speed of sound slightly (less than 0.5 % at room temperature).
  • The quarter-wave rule is a guide only. It is not an exact limit.

References: L. E. Kinsler et al., Fundamentals of Acoustics. ISO 9613-1 (atmospheric properties).
