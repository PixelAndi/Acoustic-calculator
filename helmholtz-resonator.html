Single Helmholtz resonator

This calculator gives the resonance frequency of one resonator: a closed box (or bottle) with a neck. Use it to design a tuned bass absorber for one room mode.

How to use

  1. Select what you want to find: the frequency or the volume.
  2. Type the neck diameter and the neck length. The neck length is the thickness of the wall with the hole, or the length of the tube.
  3. Select the type of the outer end of the neck.
  4. Type the internal volume of the box, or the target frequency.
  5. Find the room modes with the room modes calculator. Tune the resonator to the mode that causes the problem.

Description

The air in the neck is a mass. The air in the box is a spring. At the resonance frequency the air in the neck moves strongly. Porous material in the box or a resistive cloth over the neck changes this energy into heat. The absorption band is narrow.

Method

f₀ = (c / 2π) √(S / (V L′)), S = π a² L′ = L + δ_inner + δ_outer δ = 0.85 a (end in a wall or flange, Rayleigh) δ = 0.61 a (end of a free tube, Levine & Schwinger) Volume for a target f₀: V = S / (L′ (2π f₀ / c)²)

a is the neck radius and V is the internal volume of the box. The inner end is inside the box and is usually flanged.

Sources: resonance formula ; flanged end correction 8a/(3π) ≈ 0.85a ; unflanged end correction 0.6133a .

Limits

  • All dimensions of the box and the neck must be much smaller than the wavelength. The calculator shows a warning if the largest dimension is more than 1/8 of the wavelength.
  • The formula gives the frequency only. The absorption depends on the damping and on the position in the room. Put the resonator where the room mode has high pressure (for example, in a corner).
  • Porous material in the box lowers the frequency a little.
