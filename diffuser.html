How to use

  1. Select the type: QRD (1D wells), PRD (1D wells) or skyline (2D blocks).
  2. Select the prime number N. A larger N gives more wells and usually better diffusion.
  3. Type the design frequency. The diffuser works well from approximately this frequency.
  4. Type the well width (or block width). This sets the highest useful frequency.
  5. Read the depths and the diffusion coefficient. Change the inputs until the coefficient is high in the band that you need.

Description

A diffuser scatters sound in many directions. It removes strong reflections and echoes but keeps the sound energy in the room. A Schroeder diffuser has wells (or blocks) of different depths. Each depth gives a different delay to the reflected sound. The sequence of depths comes from number theory, so the scattered energy is spread evenly.

  • QRD (quadratic residue): s_n = n² mod N. It scatters in one plane.
  • PRD (primitive root): s_n = gⁿ mod N. It decreases the reflection in the specular direction at the design frequency.
  • Skyline: a grid of blocks with s = (n² + m²) mod N. It scatters in all directions.

Method

Depth: d_n = s_n λ₀ / (2N), λ₀ = c / f₀ Highest useful frequency: f_max ≈ c / (2w) (w = well width) Flat-surface frequencies: f = m · N · f₀ (all wells reflect in phase) Scattering (far field, normal incidence, Kirchhoff / Fourier model): p(θ) ∝ Σ R_n w_n sinc(k w_n sin θ / 2) exp(j k x_n sin θ), R_n = exp(−2jk d_n) Diffusion coefficient (ISO 17497-2 form), 37 directions, 5° steps: d = [(Σ E_i)² − Σ E_i²] / [(n − 1) Σ E_i²] Normalised: d_n = (d − d_flat) / (1 − d_flat)

For the skyline diffuser, the chart shows the scattering in the horizontal plane. The calculation uses the mean reflection factor of each column of blocks.

Limits

  • The scattering model is the Fourier (Kirchhoff) model of Cox & D'Antonio. It is a far-field estimate for normal incidence. It does not include the absorption in the wells or the coupling between wells. It is less accurate at low frequencies.
  • Above f_max, sound can move across the width of a well, and the depth sequence works less well.
  • At the flat-surface frequencies, the diffuser reflects like a flat surface. Then the diffusion coefficient decreases.
  • Narrow, deep wells absorb some sound, mostly at low frequencies. Seal and finish the wells well.

References: M. R. Schroeder, J. Acoust. Soc. Am. 57 (1975) 149–150 and 65 (1979) 958–963. T. J. Cox & P. D'Antonio, Acoustic Absorbers and Diffusers, chapters 9–10. ISO 17497-2, measurement of the directional diffusion coefficient.
