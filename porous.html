Porous absorber

This calculator gives the sound absorption of a porous layer on a rigid wall. The layer can be mineral wool, glass wool or open-cell foam. You can add an air gap between the layer and the wall.

How to use

  1. Type the thickness of the porous layer.
  2. Type the flow resistivity of the material. Get this value from the data sheet of the material.
  3. Type the air gap between the layer and the wall. Type 0 if the layer touches the wall.
  4. Select the incident sound. Use "Diffuse" for a normal room.
  5. Read the curve and the band table. To compare two designs, click "Keep curve for comparison" and then change the inputs.

Description

A porous material has many small open channels. Sound moves the air in these channels. Friction between the air and the fibres changes the sound energy into heat.

Air moves fastest at a quarter wavelength from a rigid wall. Thus a thick layer, or a layer with an air gap behind it, absorbs lower frequencies. A thin layer on the wall absorbs only high frequencies.

Flow resistivity (σ) tells how much the material resists a flow of air. If σ is too low, the material does not cause sufficient friction. If σ is too high, sound reflects from the surface. For many building products, σ is between 5 000 and 50 000 Pa·s/m².

Method

The calculator uses the Delany & Bazley or the Miki model to get the characteristic impedance Zc and the wavenumber k of the material. It then moves the impedance from the wall to the surface, one layer at a time (transfer of impedance).

X = ρ₀ f / σ Z_c = ρ₀c₀ [1 + 0.0571 X^-0.754 − j 0.087 X^-0.732] k = (ω/c₀)[1 + 0.0978 X^-0.700 − j 0.189 X^-0.595] Layer on rigid wall: Z = −j Z_n cot(k_x d) Next layer (load Z_L): Z = Z_n (Z_L cos k_x d + j Z_n sin k_x d) / (Z_n cos k_x d + j Z_L sin k_x d) Oblique sound: k_x = √(k² − k₀² sin²θ), Z_n = Z_c k / k_x Reflection: R = (Z cosθ − ρ₀c₀) / (Z cosθ + ρ₀c₀), α = 1 − |R|² Diffuse field (Paris): α_d = ∫₀^{π/2} α(θ) sin 2θ dθ

Miki uses the same form with f/σ and the coefficients 0.070, 0.107 (exponent −0.632) and 0.109, 0.160 (exponent −0.618).

Limits

  • The models are empirical. They are accurate for 0.01 ≤ X ≤ 1.0. The calculator shows a warning outside this range.
  • The Delany & Bazley model can give negative absorption at low frequencies. This is an error of the model. Use the Miki model for these frequencies.
  • The calculation is for a layer of infinite size. Small panels absorb more sound per square metre because of edge diffraction. Laboratory values can be higher than the values here.
  • The wall is rigid. The frame of the material does not move.
  • Without partitions, the layer is "extended reacting": sound can travel along the layer. Select "Cavity has partitions" only if the layer is divided into small cells.
