How to use

  1. Type the length, width and height of the room.
  2. Select how you know the room now: the mean absorption coefficient, or a measured reverberation time.
  3. Type these values for each octave band.
  4. Type the total area of the panels and their absorption coefficients. To use a result from a panel page, click "Save result for other pages" on that page. Then click "Load last panel result" here.
  5. For hanging objects (clouds, baffles, furniture), type their equivalent absorption area in the column "Extra A". The clouds and baffles page fills this column.
  6. Read the reverberation time before and after.

Description

Reverberation time (RT60) is the time for the sound level to decrease by 60 dB after the source stops. A long RT60 makes speech difficult to understand. Absorbers decrease the RT60.

The equivalent absorption area A is the sum of (surface area × absorption coefficient) for all surfaces. The unit is m².

Method

Sabine: T = 55.26 V / (c A) Eyring: T = 55.26 V / (−c S ln(1 − ᾱ)), ᾱ = A / S From a measured T: A = 55.26 V / (c T) After: A′ = A − S_p ᾱ + S_p α_p + A_extra

V is the room volume, S is the total surface area and S_p is the panel area. The constant 55.26 is 24 ln 10. The panels cover an area of the room that had the mean absorption coefficient ᾱ.

Limits

  • The formulas are correct for a diffuse sound field. They are less accurate in a room with one very absorbent surface, or in a long or flat room.
  • The tool does not include air absorption. Air absorption decreases the RT60 above approximately 2 kHz in large rooms.
  • People and furniture add absorption. Include them in the mean absorption coefficient, or use a measured RT60.
  • Use diffuse-field (random-incidence) values for the panels.

References: W. C. Sabine, Collected Papers on Acoustics (1922). C. F. Eyring, J. Acoust. Soc. Am. 1 (1930) 217–241. ISO 3382-2 (measurement of reverberation time).
