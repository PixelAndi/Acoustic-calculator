Multilayer and broadband absorber

Build your own absorber from layers. Use this page for broadband panels, fabric-covered panels, panels with two porous materials, double microperforated panels and other combinations.

How to use

  1. Select a preset, or start with the default layers.
  2. Change the layers. Layer 1 is on the room side. The last layer is next to the wall.
  3. Click "Add layer" to add a layer. Use the arrows to move a layer. Click "×" to remove a layer.
  4. Select the back: a rigid wall, or open air (for a panel that hangs free).
  5. Read the curve. Click "Keep curve for comparison" to compare two designs.

Description

A broadband absorber absorbs well over a wide range of frequencies. Usually it is a thick porous layer with an air gap behind it. A thin fabric on the front protects the porous layer and has almost no effect on the absorption if its flow resistance is low.

You can add sheets between layers. A perforated, slotted or microperforated sheet adds a resonance and moves absorption to lower frequencies. A closed sheet (membrane) makes a bass absorber.

Layer types

  • Fabric or scrim: a thin porous sheet. Flow resistance R (Pa·s/m) and surface mass. If the fabric is stretched or bonded, type mass 0.
  • Porous layer: mineral wool, glass wool, foam or felt. Thickness and flow resistivity σ.
  • Air gap: an empty space.
  • Perforated, slotted, microperforated sheet: the same models as on their own pages.
  • Membrane: a closed limp sheet with a surface mass.

Method

Wall backing: impedance transfer from the wall, layer by layer Z_in = Z_n (Z_L cos k_x d + j Z_n sin k_x d) / (Z_n cos k_x d + j Z_L sin k_x d) Sheet: Z_in = Z_L + Z_sheet Open backing: transfer matrix of all layers, air on both sides α = 1 − |R|² − |t|² (energy that goes through is not absorbed) Fabric (limp): Z = jωm R / (R + jωm); m = 0 → Z = R

Limits

  • All layers are flat, of infinite size, and the layers do not vibrate (except the membrane and the limp fabric).
  • A sheet directly against the rigid wall has no effect. The calculator shows a warning.
  • When a porous layer touches the back of a slotted or perforated sheet, real products can absorb more than this model shows. Use measured data if it is available.
  • The porous models are accurate for 0.01 ≤ X ≤ 1.0. The calculator shows a warning outside this range.
  • With an open back, the result is for sound that comes from the front only. For a hanging baffle in a room, use the clouds and baffles page.

References: T. J. Cox & P. D'Antonio, Acoustic Absorbers and Diffusers, chapters 2, 5–7 (transfer matrix and impedance models). J. F. Allard & N. Atalla, Propagation of Sound in Porous Media (transfer matrices).
